Events, with intent.

A small TypeScript event emitter with wildcards, priorities, middleware, flow control and history, plus WebSocket and Server-Sent Events adapters.

  • No dependencies
  • TypeScript
  • ESM

Features

Each example runs the real library: press its button to see what EvEm does, open its code, or take it to the playground to change it.

Wildcards

One subscription for a family of events: user.* for everything about users, * for everything. Type your own pattern and see what it matches, and why.

Priorities

Subscribers run highest priority first, so an audit log sees each event before any other subscriber does. Change the priorities and publish again.

Middleware

Middleware sees every event before its subscribers do: it can change the data, cancel the event, or send it under another name.

Throttle & debounce

Keep a fast stream from flooding a slow subscriber: throttle runs it at most once per window, debounce waits for a pause, and together they do both. The chart shows a burst of keystrokes over time.

Cancelable events

Publish an event as cancelable and a subscriber can cancel it: the subscribers after it don't run. A fraud check that stops a payment before it's charged.

History & replay

EvEm can remember events, so a subscriber that arrives late still gets the latest one, or all of them.

Schema validation

Give a subscriber a schema and EvEm checks the data before calling it. By default, invalid data skips that subscriber and is logged; other policies skip it quietly, run it anyway, or reject the publish.

Adapters

Connect EvEm to a server, and its messages arrive as events. These cards run the real adapters against a server in the page: drop its connection and watch them recover.

WebSocket

WebSocketHandler sends what you publish on ws.send while connected, and queues it while not. Drop the connection, keep sending, and watch it reconnect and flush the queue.

Server-Sent Events

SseHandler turns a stream into events. Drop the stream: it reconnects and sends Last-Event-ID, so the ticks go on where they stopped.

Why EvEm

Most emitters stop at subscribe and emit. EvEm adds what apps end up building around them, awaits async subscribers in priority order, and has no dependencies. It isn't always the right pick:

Next to Node's EventEmitter

EvEm adds wildcards, priorities, middleware, filters, throttle and debounce, history, and async subscribers awaited in order with timeouts; and it runs in browsers too.

Pick EventEmitter for what's built into Node, with a synchronous emit.

Next to mitt or EventEmitter3

EvEm adds patterns, priorities, middleware, history and error policies, and tracks subscriptions by id.

Pick them when bundle size or raw speed matters most, or for a typed event map: EvEm types payloads per call, not per event name.

Next to RxJS

EvEm is plain publish and subscribe: smaller, and quicker to learn.

Pick RxJS for composable operators over streams and complex async workflows.

The full comparison is in the README.
